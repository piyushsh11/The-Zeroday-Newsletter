Cyber News24 Jul 2026By Srinidi Subramaniam04 min read

Bank of Baroda breach claim: one inbox, a terabyte of risk

Threat actor “TripleX” claimed access to roughly 1 TB of internal and customer data—including Aadhaar, PAN, and KYC records—through a single compromised employee email account. The bank confirmed that its core banking systems remained secure.

Read full brief

The claim illustrates how a narrow identity compromise can expose a broad data trail without breaching a bank’s transaction core. Email archives often contain customer documents, internal correspondence, attachments, and links into adjacent systems.

Defender takeaway: protect high-value mailboxes with phishing-resistant MFA, behavioral monitoring, strict session controls, and data-loss prevention. Segregating sensitive KYC workflows also reduces what one compromised identity can reach.

Data & Risk2026 ReportBy Srinidi Subramaniam03 min read

India’s data breach costs hit an all-time high

The average breach cost in India reached ₹25.5 crore, a 15.9% year-over-year increase. AI-generated techniques featured in 26% of malicious breaches, while phishing remained the leading initial access vector.

Read full brief

Rising costs reflect more than incident cleanup: detection delays, business disruption, legal response, customer notification, and reputation loss compound the impact. The figures also show that AI is amplifying familiar attacker playbooks rather than replacing them.

Defender takeaway: prioritize email authentication, rapid containment drills, endpoint visibility, tested backups, and employee reporting paths. Prevention matters, but recovery speed increasingly determines the final bill.

AI SecurityCase StudyBy Yuvraj Bishnoi04 min read

Anthropic’s Claude AI exposes challenges in secure AI testing

A misconfigured test environment—intended to be isolated—allowed Claude models to access three real-world organizations unintentionally. The incident underscores how AI evaluation environments can cross boundaries when their tools and credentials are not tightly controlled.

Read full brief

Model isolation is not only a model problem. It depends on the surrounding credentials, network routes, connectors, data stores, and tool permissions. A sandbox that retains production reach can turn exploratory model behavior into a real-world incident.

Defender takeaway: make test environments deny-by-default, use synthetic data and non-production identities, restrict egress, and log every tool call. Treat the agent harness as part of the security boundary.

Supply ChainBreach BriefBy Yuvraj Bishnoi04 min read

Amgen data breach: patient records and drug research exposed

Attackers breached a third-party cloud provider rather than Amgen directly, exposing patient health data, intellectual property, and research-and-development files. The incident was reported to the U.S. SEC.

Read full brief

Third-party hosting concentrates valuable data while separating it from the organization’s day-to-day controls. Health information and drug research carry distinct privacy, competitive, and regulatory consequences, increasing the blast radius of a provider compromise.

Defender takeaway: inventory vendor-held data, enforce least-privilege access, require auditable incident obligations, and test how quickly provider credentials can be revoked. Contractual assurance should be backed by technical verification.

Cyber ScopeExplainerBy Srinidi Subramaniam06 min read

Threat Intelligence: Staying One Step Ahead of Cyber Threats

A practical guide to moving beyond isolated indicators and understanding attacker tactics, techniques, and procedures. It connects MITRE ATT&CK, the Cyber Kill Chain, MISP, VirusTotal, and SIEM workflows into one defender’s view.

Read full brief

Indicators such as hashes and IP addresses expire quickly; attacker behavior tends to persist. TTP-based intelligence maps those behaviors to observable stages, helping teams detect campaigns even when infrastructure changes.

  • MITRE ATT&CK provides a shared language for adversary techniques.
  • Cyber Kill Chain frames the sequence from reconnaissance to action on objectives.
  • MISP and VirusTotal enrich and share indicators, while a SIEM correlates them against local telemetry.

The strongest program closes the loop: collect, contextualize, prioritize, detect, learn, and feed the result back into controls.
